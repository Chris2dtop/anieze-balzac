Coverage ledger

Twelve looks, each seen properly

Twelve distinct complete outfits, each with a front view, a turn and an uninterrupted five-second hold. Repeats and coats coming off don't count. Garments per look are chosen by Chris at the fitting. The colour shows the part where each look is shown.

Changes

Models 1 and 2 leave first in the rush (about 3:45) and come back down their aisle in a new complete outfit at 7:25 and 8:10. That gives them about 3:30 and 4:15, office trip included. Time one change at rehearsal. If it's too tight, or if 12 performers turn up, two extra performers wear L09 and L10 in the same slots.

The room · schematic, not to scale

Who goes where

Left and right as seen from the seats, facing the screen. Guests can come down any of the three aisles; which guest takes which aisle is decided at rehearsal. The rail never touches the stairs. Confirm every mark at 11:00; if the doors sit on the other side, mirror everything.

Guests · Part 2

From the back of the room down the left, centre or right aisle, decided per guest. A light follows them all the way; the audience stays dark. Security checks the invitation at the foot of the stairs. Up onto the stage, into the spotlight at the hold spot (front, turn, five seconds, real flashes), down the left stair to the floor cloakroom: coat, ticket. Then along the front to the reserved row.

The rail

Comes in empty down the left aisle to the floor cloakroom at 5:45. Leaves full back up the left aisle at about 11:10, out of the left door, to the parking corner the cinema named by the main emergency exit. Never parked in an aisle, never on stairs.

The late pair · Part 3

Wait just inside the left door and step aside for the rail; the attendant shows the cloakroom is closed. Coats on, tiptoe down the left aisle. Their seats are on the other side, and the stage is the shortcut: up the left stair, caught in the light, down the right stair, into the reserved row.

Lighting · the audience in the dark, the clothes in the light

Follow the guest

The room stays dark for the whole show. Only the stage and the person walking are lit. As each guest comes down an aisle, a light follows them; when they reach the stage, the spotlight on the hold spot takes over for their front, turn and five-second hold.

Start

House to black

Stage lit for Part 1. The audience sits in the dark from the first second.

Aisles

A light follows each guest

Left, centre or right aisle, whichever that guest uses. From the door to the stage stairs.

Stage

Spotlight on the hold spot

The brightest point in the room, where every outfit gets its front, turn and five-second hold.

End

Blackout, then the film

When both late guests sit and the music fades, the booth goes to black and starts the film.

To ask the cinema

Does the room have a follow spot (poursuite) that can track someone down the aisles, and who would operate it? If not, the guests walk the aisles in the room's low light and the spotlight on the hold spot does the work. Decide at 11:00 after seeing the room's lights.

One operator at the desk · the projectionist in the booth

Eight presses and one fade

The music never stops before the end. Between cues the operator simply plays the next file. The only silence of the evening is the final fade, and that fade is the booth's signal for blackout and film.

The operator's phone
  • Charged to 100%, airplane mode and Do Not Disturb on.
  • All 8 files downloaded to the phone, not streamed.
  • VLC (free), playlist 01 → 08, repeat one track, shuffle off, so a file never jumps ahead on its own.

  • Wired jack into the desk, plus a backup phone with the same files.

Sound effects are inside the files: the walkie crackle at 0:25 of file 1 (Security answers it), the ticking clock under file 3, the bass hit at the start of file 7. Camera flashes are real; the cinema allows them.

Part 2

File 5 is pre-mixed: piano, waltz, Afro-house, Afrobeat, melodic techno, soft pizzicato, Afro-house. Every guest walks in on a different sound, and each change is Security's cue to let the next guest down the aisle. The operator presses nothing for six minutes.

Twelve looks, each seen properly

Twelve distinct complete outfits, each with a front view, a turn and an uninterrupted five-second hold. Repeats and coats coming off don't count. Garments per look are chosen by Chris at the fitting.

Changes

Time one change at rehearsal. If it's too tight, or if 12 performers turn up, a Model 4 and a fifth model or second photographer wear L09 and L10 in the same slots.

Who goes where

Left and right as seen from the seats, facing the screen. The rail never touches the stairs. Confirm every mark at 11:00; if the doors sit on the other side, mirror everything.

Guests (Part 2)

From the back of the room down the centre aisle, the red carpet. Security checks the invitation at the bottom. Up the right stair, across the stage to the hold spot (front, turn, 5 seconds, real flashes), down the left stair to the floor cloakroom: coat, ticket. Then along the front to the reserved row.

The rail

Comes in empty down the left aisle to the floor cloakroom at 5:45. Leaves full back up the left aisle at about 11:10, out of the left door, to the parking corner the cinema named by the main emergency exit. Never in an aisle when parked, never on stairs.

The late pair

Wait just inside the left door and step aside for the rail; the attendant shows the cloakroom is closed. Coats on, tiptoe down the left aisle. Their seats are on the other side, and the stage is the shortcut: up the left stair, caught in the light, down the right stair, into the reserved row.

Régie · one operator at the desk, the projectionist in the booth

Seven presses and one fade

The music never stops before the end. Between cues the operator simply plays the next file. The only silence of the evening is the final fade, and that fade is the booth's signal for blackout and film.

The operator's phone
  • Charged to 100%, airplane mode and Do Not Disturb on.
  • All 7 files downloaded to the phone, not streamed.
